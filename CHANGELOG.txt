Version 0.31.26

A full review of the mod against the game's own code, decompiled on 19-21/11, and against the source of the physics package the game ships (Unity Physics 1.4.7, the exact version). Everything the mod only assumed about the game was checked; what was wrong is fixed here. Not tested in the game.

Joints, pistons, bearings, turret
- Letting go of a joint now works the way the game's decoupler does: the mod asks the game to break it, inside the physics tick, and the game breaks it. Until now the mod lowered the joint's strength from outside the tick, which the game ignores when "Indestructible joints" is on and which could leave a joint half-broken. In a world where joints cannot break, a piston, bearing or turret that would separate stays whole and the log says so once.
- When a separation fails (the top piece is still held some other way), the released joints are really linked back, with their strain reset. Before, the joint was gone and only its strength was given back. The failure is now seen on the tick right after the release; the 3 s window stays as a backup.
- A piece being carried while it separates now moves with the ship's spin (speed + spin x distance), not just with the ship's speed. Both bodies share the motion that keeps their total push and spin, so nothing is created.
- "Live joints" are now the game's own list of connected joints, read once per frame; joints the game broke in a crash are never counted or re-linked as the mod's.
- Joint reinforcement keeps its original value across the whole flight and also reinforces joints on loose structures.
- The turret's saved turning inertia follows the game when the game rewrites it; turrets come back correctly after a time rewind.
- Recouplers come back locked after a rewind when their game joint is still alive.
- Comment fix only: the game uses 8 solver iterations, not 4.

Mass, flight, spin
- New: [Physics] contact_inertia_scaling (off). The game turns on a setting of the physics solver that shrinks the turning part of every contact push. Inside one ship, parts that touch each other then gain or lose spin out of nothing, even in vacuum with the ship at rest. Off = in vacuum (gravity below 0.05) the mod turns that setting off; with gravity the game's value is always used. On = the game's value everywhere. Candidate for the ship spinning on its own.
- The mod's extra masses now exist only while the solver runs: real masses go back right after it, not just before the game's forces. In vacuum, a vehicle whose joints are all locked goes through the solver with its real masses.
- Thrust on a locked group is applied as on one body when the group has stabilisation or gimbal.
- Moving the centre of mass now carries the matching speed (spin x offset), so the move itself adds no motion.
- Rigid pistons and servos advance once per physics tick instead of once per frame.
- The zero gravity of the launch window now really reaches the solver; until now the game rewrote gravity every tick and the option did nothing.
- The 0.31.25 correction of the stored speed is replaced by the above.
- Log: thrust estimate and drag watch use the game's own formulas.

Cables, power, signals
- Power across slip rings is shared from the top down and never more than each circuit has; batteries on both sides charge only from spare generation, and a burnt power cable stops the bridge.
- Port directions on DataInOut ports, the LRT and the slip ring are written the way the game does, and never undone when the game resolved them itself.
- Signal values are filtered like the game does (above 1e20 and NaN are cut, negatives pass). Wireless needs SCActive and exactly one sender within 2000 m.
- Radar, LED colours and blinking, and the beacon icon follow the game's formulas.

Couplers, world, multiplayer, garage
- The 100 km rule decides per group of linked parts and always gives the part back to the physics world; the mod's joints are rebuilt in the current pose when it comes back. Turrets and recouplers are frozen with the group instead of being dropped.
- Merging parts keeps their push and spin.
- [Wheel] friction default is now 2.0. A config file that already exists keeps its value until you change it.
- The 3 s pause on unload is only for leaving the world; a recall restarts the mod on the next frame.
- Multiplayer: a player with status On counts as connected, an offline one does not. The mod channel protocol is now 2: every machine needs 0.31.26.
- Turret in multiplayer: the server sends the turret's layout, so clients draw the turret and its pieces with the server's formula. A client sitting at a turret aims from where the turret already points.
- Bonus parts go through the game's own inventory refresh. The seat shortcuts reach 4.5 m like the game (the mod read it as about 2.1 m). Painting uses the game's function. Old names are converted in the blueprints bin too. The garage ignores blueprint ghosts.
- Dead code removed (PistonMerge leftovers).

Not changed: the texts, the mass balance itself, the drag, the piston feel.

Version 0.31.25

One correction to 0.31.23, found while reading the game's strain code (SpaceshipInertiaToShockwaveSystem). The game stores each part's speed at the start of its force step and compares it with the speed after the solver to work out the jolt on every block; a jolt above about 0,17 m/s in one tick (a little over 1 g) strains the block's joints. Since 0.31.23 the hull's speed was stored while its real centre of mass was in and compared while the solver's centre was in — the same motion measured at two points 0,26 m apart, which the game would read as a small extra jolt whenever the ship is turning. The stored speed is now carried over to the solver's point before the solver runs, so the game's strain sees exactly the jolt there was. Nothing else changed.
Not tested in the game.

Version 0.31.24

What changed since 0.31.23, after reading the game's own code (its Burst code was decompiled on 18/11; the parts quoted here are SpaceshipPartsApplyForces and AerodynamicsSystem).
Confirmed: the game works out every push on a part from that part's stored mass and centre of mass — the change of speed is force x inverse mass, and the turning effect is taken about the stored centre of mass and divided by the stored inertia. So the centre of mass the mod had moved 0,26 m on the hull did put the hull's thrust 0,26 m off the ship's centre, as 0.31.23 assumed. That part of 0.31.23 stays as it is.
Corrected: 0.31.23 said "in the game a thruster never turns the part it sits on". That is wrong. The game applies the full turning effect of every thruster and then, separately, brakes the part's spin by a limited amount each tick (the thruster's stabilisation). The option built on that wrong sentence, [Physics] welded_thrust_through_centre, is removed; it was off by default and never ran.
New: [Physics] welded_drag_as_one (on). The game's drag never turns a part: each part gets one braking number, and its speed and spin are both scaled by it; the number comes from rays cast against that part's own collider only. A ship in one piece just slows down. The same ship with a gate on pistons has each gate piece braked on its own, far from the centre, and that difference turns the ship in the air — which is what Andre found on 14/11. Now a welded group is braked as one part: what each of its parts received is undone and the group is slowed by the mass-weighted mean, speed and spin alike. The total braking is what the game applied; only the turning it caused is gone. Known limit: a gate piece hidden behind the hull still counts its own area, so the ship brakes a little more than the one-piece ship would.
Log: the momentum watch line says how much the group was slowed per tick by that rule.
Not tested in the game.

Version 0.31.21

Fixed: the ship gaining spin on its own. Measured on 14/11: coasting in vacuum with every engine off, Andre's ship went from 0 to 0,27 degrees per second in 44 seconds, twice — a vehicle touching nothing outside itself cannot do that, so the mod's own joints were making it.
How it is found without guessing which joint: the spin momentum of the whole vehicle is read at the END of PhysicsPreGroup — the game's thrust and air have been applied by then — and again at the START of the group after the physics solver. Everything the engines and the air did is inside the first reading and drops out of the subtraction. What is left crossed the solver, where the mod's welds, servos and pistons act, and for a vehicle touching nothing outside, that cannot be anything but internal. [Physics] keep_spin (on) takes exactly that back out, as one rigid correction spread over every part of the vehicle: the same extra angular velocity on all of them plus the matching linear velocity, so no piece is pushed against another and the vehicle's linear momentum is untouched. It works with the engines running, which is where the problem actually shows up.
Guards, so it does not become a cheat: only with no air (in the atmosphere or on the ground the vehicle leans on things the mod cannot account for), and only on a slow drift — [Physics] keep_spin_max (0,2 degrees per second of ship spin per second of flight). Anything faster is a jolt from outside, and a hit, a block coming off or a decoupler firing goes through untouched.
Log: the momentum watch line now also says how much momentum crossed the solver per second (the leak itself, measured with the ship flying normally, engines on) and how much keep_spin took back, plus how many jolts it left alone.
Nothing else changed: the gate, the door, the pistons, the servos and the mass balance are exactly as they were.

Version 0.31.18

Fixed (or at least: changed, with the number that tells us): how much harder to TURN than it really is a piece may be counted. While a piston weld holds two parts, the lighter one is counted heavier so the solver listens to the weld (mass_ratio_max, since 0.26.7), and its turning inertia was counted heavier too — capped at mass_factor_max SQUARED, 625x, which was never a decision, it just fell out of "inertia goes with size squared". A piece counted 25x heavier and 625x harder to turn trades momentum with the hull through the weld at a made-up ratio; on Andre's ship there are six of those around the gate (63 to 80 kg pieces counted as 500 to 1570 kg), and the ship leans in vacuum, where nothing outside it can be pushing. The inertia now has its own cap, [Piston] inertia_factor_max, at 25x. Set it to 625 for the old behaviour if a piston starts sagging under weight.
New (log only): momentum watch, [Debug] momentum_watch, on by default and needing no special flight. A vehicle flying with no air can only have its spin changed from outside, by its thrusters: the welds, the servos, the pistons and the car resting on the floor only pass momentum from one piece to another, they cannot create it. Every half second the mod adds up the spin momentum of every part of the vehicle and logs how fast the total drifts, and what that drift is worth as degrees per second added to the hull each second. Drifting with the thrusters off means the mod is making spin out of nothing, and the number says how much; not drifting means the lean comes from outside the vehicle and the mod is not the cause. The line also goes into the black box snapshot (F8), and it says when the mod changed the masses in that window, because then a jump in the total is expected.
Note: the drag watch of 0.31.17 answered one thing already — in space the air density is EXACTLY zero, so the game applies no drag at all up there, and the lean survived with the drag switched off. Whatever turns the ship in vacuum, it is not the game's air.

Version 0.31.17

New (log only, debug): drag watch, [Debug] drag_watch. Two flights with the same ship, one with the microframe gate and one with that opening closed by a welded wall, pointed at the game's own drag: with the gate the ship starts to lean in space and ends up tumbling at 86 deg/s, without it it holds 0,3 deg/s at 3000 m/s — while making six times MORE thruster torque, which the game cancels on its own. So something the game does not compensate is turning the ship, and the gate is what changes. The game computes drag per PART, casting rays against that part's own shape, so a ship with a gate is several bodies taking the wind instead of one: the hull with the whole doorway missing from its shape, the door as a body of its own pushing the hull through the bearing, and the gap around the door that can never be closed. Every 0.5 s this writes one "drag watch:" line: the air density with real decimals (the flight line rounds it to 0.000, which hides what is left in high orbit) and, per part, the two numbers the game keeps for its drag plus how much that part's speed and spin changed in the step the drag runs in. A part with no thruster pushing it in that tick gets nothing else in that step, so for that one the change IS the drag.
New (test only): [Debug] no_drag. Zeroes the game's drag on every part, every tick, to tell apart "the gate's drag is turning my ship" from "something else is": fly the same ship with it on and off. It is NOT a way to play — with it on nothing in flight is slowed by the air, so a bad manoeuvre or a pass near the sun stops costing what it should. The log warns once while it is on, and says whether the game accepted the zero or put its own numbers back in the same tick; if it put them back, the switch turns the drag off the other way instead — it takes the air away from each part where the air is already thinner than 0.01, for the one step the game's drag runs in, and gives it back right after. Real atmosphere is left alone, so the switch only turns the drag off high up and in space.
Nothing in the flight changes with both switches off (they are off by default).

[Slip Ring] rebuild_circuits_in_tick (on by default; the log says once whether it is working).
- When a joint was born, split, merged or got a new part, the ring's link was cut and only made again on the next half-second scan (in Andre's log: gone at 22:20:38.117, back at 38.633, with everything behind that piston dark in between). Now the rings are scanned in the very tick a joint, recoupler or turret changes, and a link whose ring is not found in one scan stays up for a second instead of being cut on the spot (unless another joint took that ring, or it is a recoupler, whose links go by distance).
- Launch: the links are made as the joints are born, instead of after two half-second clocks in a row. A chain of joints, which settles one joint per pass, now settles in consecutive ticks.
- A side of a bridge that lost its circuit and did not find the new one at once is looked for again on every tick (it waited for the next frame).
New (log only): power watch, [Debug] power_watch (on by default). Every game tick, every circuit fed through a slip ring and every electric thruster is checked for a component that asks for power and is not supplied. One "power watch:" line per episode: what, for how many ticks, and what was happening in the tick it began (the game rebuilt a circuit, a joint changed, the feeding side had no power, or nothing). The trace samples the thrusters every 2 s; a cut shorter than that could not be seen.
Log: the performance line also counts ring scans on a joint change, data links the game reset and the mod put back, and the power watch's episodes.
Not done: a reserve of stored power on the bridge. The moment that matters is the one in which the bridge's entity is not in the circuit, so what it holds is not there either, and accounting for what the game takes from it depends on game behaviour that could not be checked (a wrong guess drains the ship). The reserve that works is a battery of the game on the fed side.
